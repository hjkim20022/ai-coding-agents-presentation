# Video Script

## Presentation Title

**The Rise of AI Coding Agents in 2026: Helpful Learning Tools or a Risk of Overdependence?**

Estimated speaking time: approximately 2 minutes and 40 seconds.

---

## Student 1 — Introduction, Current Event, and Benefits

Hello. We are HeeJung Kim and Arif Azmi.

Our topic is the rise of AI coding agents in 2026 and whether they are helpful learning tools or a risk of overdependence.

Recent developments such as the OpenAI Codex app and GitHub Copilot Agent show that AI coding tools are becoming more powerful. Unlike basic autocomplete, an AI coding agent can examine project files, propose changes, run commands, test code, and help complete multistep programming tasks.

These agents offer several benefits. They can explain unfamiliar code, identify possible errors, and help programmers complete repetitive work more quickly. For beginners, they can provide examples and personalized explanations.

For example, a student could ask an AI agent to explain a Python loop, identify an error, and suggest a correction. The student can then run the code and compare the result with the explanation.

However, faster completion does not always mean better learning.

---

## Student 2 — Risks, Responsible Use, and Conclusion

The main risk is overdependence. Students may copy AI-generated code without understanding how it works. This can weaken independent problem-solving and make it difficult to recognize incorrect or insecure code.

There are also concerns about privacy and academic integrity. Users should not upload sensitive information, and students must follow their instructor’s rules.

The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 explains that generative AI can support learning when it is guided by clear teaching principles. However, using AI simply to complete tasks may improve immediate performance without producing genuine learning.

A balanced approach is to use AI as a tutor, not as a replacement for thinking. Students should ask for explanations, test the code, identify mistakes, and make sure they can explain their final solution.

In conclusion, AI coding agents can improve productivity and support learning, but responsible human judgment remains essential.

Our discussion question is:

**If AI coding agents help students finish programs faster but may reduce independent problem-solving, how should schools decide what level of AI assistance is acceptable?**
